# Specialized Lending Exposures

This node covers specialized lending (SL) as a subcategory of corporate exposures in SAMA's credit risk capital framework, comprising project finance, object finance, commodities finance, income-producing real estate and high-volatility commercial real estate. It is characterised by the borrower being an entity with little independent repayment capacity, with repayment driven by the financed asset's income and the lender holding substantial control over the asset. It is relevant to banks under both the standardized approach (including fixed risk weights where no issue-specific rating exists) and the IRB supervisory slotting approach.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

### [[SAMA — Issue-specific and Issuer Ratings|Issue-specific and Issuer Ratings]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** The practical question is whether a specialised lending exposure can be risk-weighted off an external rating at all, or must fall into the supervisory slotting categories. The issue-specific and issuer rating rules set the threshold test for when an exposure is treated as rated (and when an issuer rating may or may not be extended to a particular claim), which feeds the specialised lending treatment for project, object, commodities finance, income-producing and high-volatility commercial real estate. Consequently, a bank cannot apply a favourable rating-based weight to a specialised lending exposure unless the rating attaches to that exposure under those rules; otherwise the slotting weights and the associated expected loss treatment apply.
