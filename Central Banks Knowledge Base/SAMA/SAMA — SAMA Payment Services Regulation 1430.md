# SAMA Payment Services Regulation 1430

The Implementing Regulation of the Payments and Payment Services Law (Royal Decree M/26), issued in Arabic by SAMA circular; it replaces the Rules Regulating Payment Service Providers from its issuance date, with a corrective-action plan required within six months. It sets definitions (including control, holding company, complaint, licence), licensing and scope requirements for payment service providers, rules on agents and e-money distributors, outsourcing/audit/risk management obligations, customer protection and financial inclusion, contract and information disclosure duties, e-money issuance and redemption, safeguarding of protected funds, account information and payment initiation services, designation and oversight of payment systems under the PFMI, settlement finality and insolvency, supervision and enforcement, and complaints and disputes. It binds licensed payment service providers and payment system operators subject to the Law.

**Regimes:** payments, consumer protection, governance/risk

### [[SAMA — Law of Payments and Payment Services|Law of Payments and Payment Services]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether an activity is licensable payment activity, start from the Law of Payments and Payment Services, which supplies the statutory definition of "Payment Services" (execution and processing of payment transactions, payment instruments, e-money and payment wallets), and read the SAMA Payment Services Regulation 1430 as the subordinate instrument operationalising that definition. The Regulation cannot narrow the Law's scope, so licensing perimeter and defined-term questions resolve against the Law first and the Regulation second. Practically, a service captured by the statutory definition remains in scope even where the Regulation's detailed provisions are silent, and obligations should be built on the statutory term rather than the Regulation's shorthand.

### [[SAMA — Protection and Safeguarding of Protected Funds|Protection and Safeguarding of Protected Funds]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** The safeguarding provision is an internal component of the Payment Services Regulation, so a PSP's handling of customer money is governed by the defined term "Protected Funds" as used across that Regulation rather than by general trust or deposit concepts. Any decision about commingling, placement or use of customer balances must be tested against that definition and the segregation duties attached to it. Consequence: scope questions turn on whether the money meets the Protected Funds definition; if it does, the safeguarding obligations bite regardless of how the PSP labels the account internally.
