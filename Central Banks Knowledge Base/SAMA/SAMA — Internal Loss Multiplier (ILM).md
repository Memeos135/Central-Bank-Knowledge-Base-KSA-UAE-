# Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM)

The Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM) scales the Business Indicator Component up or down according to a bank's own operational loss experience, equalling one where the Loss Component and BIC are equal. It requires ten years of high-quality annual loss data, with a transitional minimum of five years subject to SAMA approval; banks below five years must either use the BIC alone or obtain SAMA's direction on using shorter data or an ILM above one. Banks must also disclose whether losses are used in the ILM and why not, where applicable.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** If you are testing whether a bank's operational risk capital has been correctly calibrated, the ILM cannot be verified without the Loss Component, since the ILM is defined as a function of the LC relative to the Business Indicator Component and equals one where the two are equal. The LC itself is fixed by a multiple of average annual operational risk losses over the preceding ten-year window, so the loss data set drives the multiplier. Consequently, challenges to the capital figure will usually turn on the completeness, quality and ten-year coverage of the internal loss data feeding the LC.
