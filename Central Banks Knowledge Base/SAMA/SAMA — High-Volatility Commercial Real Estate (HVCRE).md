# High-Volatility Commercial Real Estate (HVCRE)

Defines High-Volatility Commercial Real Estate (HVCRE) as a sub-category of specialised lending within the IRB credit risk framework, distinguished from Income-Producing Real Estate (IPRE) by higher loss-rate volatility and asset correlation. It captures commercial real estate exposures in property types SAMA designates as volatile, plus land acquisition, development and construction (ADC) financing where repayment depends on uncertain future sale or cash flows. Binds SAMA-licensed banks for capital calculation and, via the related disclosure definitions (General/Income-producing RRE and CRE, ADC), for Pillar 3 reporting.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** Classifying a facility as HVCRE is not merely descriptive: it determines which capital treatment applies, because the supervisory slotting provisions set distinct risk weights and expected-loss outcomes for HVCRE separately from other specialised lending categories. The HVCRE definition, resting on higher loss-rate volatility in commercial real estate financing, is the defined term that triggers that separate column of the slotting framework. The reader should therefore resolve the HVCRE characterisation first, since a misclassification as ordinary income-producing real estate changes the applicable weights and understates capital.
