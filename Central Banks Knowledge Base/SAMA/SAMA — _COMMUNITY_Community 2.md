# Payment Services Regulation

## Why this community

The core prudential and conduct architecture for banks, payment systems and payment service providers in KSA: licensing, capital, safeguarding of client funds, agents and outsourcing, systemic payment infrastructure oversight, and the consumer-facing conduct layer (framework contracts, unauthorised transactions, complaints, bancassurance, savings products, financial inclusion).

## How members connect

- Clear hierarchy: primary laws (Central Bank Law, Banking Control Law, Law of Payments and Payment Services, Currency Law, Credit Information Law, Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law) feed implementing regulations (Implementing Regulation of the Law of Payments, SAMA Payment Services Regulation, Implementation Rules for the Banking Control Law) and then licensing guidelines and circulars.
- Obligation chains radiate from the Payment Services Regulation: capital, protected-funds safeguarding, agent appointment, e-money distribution, outsourcing, framework contracts, AIS/PIS, unauthorised-transaction liability and complaints all attach to the same defined 'payment service provider' and 'payment service user' terms.
- Payment-system oversight branch runs law to supervisory framework to international benchmarks (PFMI) via self-assessment and supervisory assessment, keyed to systemic importance designation.
- Banking-side members (large exposures, related parties, loans-to-deposits, Shariah governance and Shari'ah-compliant capital add-ons, IFSB standards, branch network policy) share the Banking Control Law/Central Bank Law root, while bancassurance links the banking and insurance regimes through bank and company obligations under a single agreement framework.
