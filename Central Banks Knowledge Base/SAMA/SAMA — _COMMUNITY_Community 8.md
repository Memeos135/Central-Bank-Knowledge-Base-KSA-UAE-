# AML & Terrorism Financing Law

## Why this community

The core KSA AML/CTF statutory regime: the AML Law and the CTF Law, the preventive measures they impose on financial institutions and DNFBPs, the powers of supervisory authorities and the FIU, confiscation, and the official data sources used to verify beneficial ownership.

## How members connect

- Two parallel statutes (AML Law and Law on Combating the Financing of Terrorism) share the same architecture and cross-refer: preventive measures for FIs/DNFBPs, supervisory authority powers, FIU reporting, and confiscation.
- Preventive-measures provisions form an obligation chain: CDD and beneficial owner identification duties feed suspicious transaction reporting to the General Directorate of Financial Intelligence, enforced through supervisory authority powers and sanctions.
- Watheq, the non-profit associations beneficial owner inquiry service and Awqaf services are operational verification channels that derive their legal basis from the AML Law's beneficial owner requirements - useful for evidencing verification, not substitutes for the statutory duty.
- Data-sharing items (e.g. residential real estate finance data with SIMAH) sit at the intersection of AML record-keeping/verification and the credit information regime, so confidentiality and permitted-purpose limits in both regimes must be read together.
- Note the defined-term boundary: 'Customer' under AML differs from client/consumer concepts in BNPL and consumer protection rules, which affects who must be subjected to CDD.
