# Equity Investments in Funds

This node covers the capital treatment of equity investments in funds, setting out the look-through approach (LTA), mandate-based approach (MBA) and fall-back approach, treatment of funds-of-funds, partial use, and leverage adjustment; the related material also defines what counts as an equity holding and the 250%/400% risk weights for equity exposures. It governs how banks risk-weight fund holdings depending on the transparency of the fund's underlying exposures. It binds SAMA-supervised banks under both the standardised and IRB approaches.

**Regimes:** banking prudential

### [[SAMA — Look-Through Approach (LTA)|Look-Through Approach (LTA)]] — `shares_data_with` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** If a bank holds equity investments in funds in the banking book, the choice of capital treatment is made within a single hierarchy, and the look-through approach is the first and most risk-sensitive option in that hierarchy. The two provisions sit in the same instrument: the equity-investments-in-funds rules set the scope and the conditions for selecting among the look-through, mandate-based and fall-back approaches, and the LTA provisions supply the mechanics, including leverage adjustment and interaction with IRB. The practical consequence is that eligibility for LTA depends on sufficient and verifiable information about the fund's underlying exposures; where that is unavailable, the bank falls down the hierarchy to a more conservative approach. Read the two sections together rather than as independent rules.

### [[SAMA — Mandate-Based Approach (MBA)|Mandate-Based Approach (MBA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** For each banking book fund holding you must work down a prescribed hierarchy of approaches, and the mandate-based approach is the middle tier used where full look-through is not available but the fund's mandate or applicable regulation supports a conservative reconstruction of exposures. The equity-investments-in-funds provisions establish the three approaches and their ordering, with the fall-back approach applying where neither look-through nor mandate information suffices, and leverage adjustment and partial-use rules attaching to the choice. The consequence is that MBA is not freely elective: the bank must be able to show why look-through was unavailable, or the more punitive fall-back treatment applies.

### [[SAMA — SEC-ERBA|SEC-ERBA]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a bank holds equity in a fund and looks through to the underlying holdings, any securitisation exposures inside that fund must be capitalised under the securitisation framework rather than as plain equity. The fund provisions cross-refer to the securitisation approach hierarchy, under which SEC-ERBA is the external-ratings-based approach applied where the bank does not use (or is not permitted to use) SEC-IRBA or SEC-IAA. The practical consequence is that the look-through result is only as good as the securitisation approach selected for each underlying position, and the eligibility conditions for that approach must be met and evidenced.
