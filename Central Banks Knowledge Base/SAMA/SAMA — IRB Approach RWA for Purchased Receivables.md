# IRB Approach: RWA for Purchased Receivables

Concept node for the IRB calculation of risk-weighted assets on purchased receivables, covering default risk and dilution risk components, treatment of purchase price discounts, and recognition of credit risk mitigants. It forms part of SAMA's minimum capital requirements for credit risk and feeds the related Pillar 3 RWA disclosure rows. It binds banks using IRB approaches for eligible purchased receivables.

**Regimes:** banking prudential

### [[SAMA — Dilution Risk|Dilution Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If a bank holds purchased receivables under the IRB approach, the decision point is that capital must be held for two distinct risks, not one: default risk on the obligors and dilution risk arising from reductions in the receivable amount through credit notes, offsets, returns and similar events. The RWA rules for purchased receivables carry a separate dilution risk computation, so the defined term governs the scope of a standalone capital charge within that same framework. A bank that capitalises only default risk on a purchased receivables pool is under-capitalised unless the applicable exemption conditions in the primary text are met.

### [[SAMA — Securitization Framework|Securitization Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a bank applies the IRB treatment for purchased receivables, it needs to test whether the receivables pool is in substance a securitisation, because the two chapters lead to different RWA outcomes. The purchased receivables rules use the IRB asset-class list (including corporate purchased receivables), while the securitisation chapter supplies its own IRB route with KIRB, attachment/detachment points and the supervisory parameter — and KIRB itself is derived from the underlying IRB capital on the pool, creating a direct obligation chain between the two. Consequence for the reader: decide the characterisation before choosing the approach, and where a pool is tranched, expect the securitisation chapter to govern and to require the underlying IRB inputs as a precondition.
