# Mandate-Based Approach (MBA)

The Mandate-Based Approach is one of three hierarchical methods (alongside the look-through approach and the fall-back approach) for risk-weighting a bank's equity investments in funds under the SAMA credit risk framework. It allows a bank that cannot look through to a fund's actual holdings to assign risk weights on the basis of the fund's mandate or prospectus limits, and interacts with leverage adjustment rules, partial use, funds investing in other funds, and application by IRB banks. It binds SAMA-licensed banks calculating credit RWA and is also referenced in Pillar 3 disclosure templates.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** For each banking book fund holding you must work down a prescribed hierarchy of approaches, and the mandate-based approach is the middle tier used where full look-through is not available but the fund's mandate or applicable regulation supports a conservative reconstruction of exposures. The equity-investments-in-funds provisions establish the three approaches and their ordering, with the fall-back approach applying where neither look-through nor mandate information suffices, and leverage adjustment and partial-use rules attaching to the choice. The consequence is that MBA is not freely elective: the bank must be able to show why look-through was unavailable, or the more punitive fall-back treatment applies.

### [[SAMA — Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR)|Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Where a bank capitalises an equity investment in a fund using the mandate-based approach, derivative positions permitted by the fund's mandate must still be valued for exposure purposes under SA-CCR, so the counterparty credit risk rules feed directly into the fund look-through calculation. Both sit within the same capital adequacy framework, and the MBA provisions rely on the SA-CCR exposure-at-default definition rather than a separate measure. The practical consequence is that a bank cannot avoid SA-CCR mechanics by holding derivatives indirectly through a fund; the mandate ceiling is converted into exposure using the same methodology.
