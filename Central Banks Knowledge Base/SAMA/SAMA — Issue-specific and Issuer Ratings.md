# Issue-specific and Issuer Ratings

Explains how banks determine whether an exposure is rated, distinguishing issue-specific ratings from issuer ratings: issuer ratings apply to senior unsecured claims only, low-quality ratings contaminate pari passu and subordinated exposures, and restricted-class ratings (e.g. deposit or counterparty ratings) apply only within that class. Also requires the rating to reflect the full amount owed (principal and interest) and bars double-counting where credit enhancement is already embedded in the issue rating. Binds banks applying the standardised approach; the related material also addresses classification of equity exposures.

**Regimes:** banking prudential

- **What this link tells you:** The practical question is whether a specialised lending exposure can be risk-weighted off an external rating at all, or must fall into the supervisory slotting categories. The issue-specific and issuer rating rules set the threshold test for when an exposure is treated as rated (and when an issuer rating may or may not be extended to a particular claim), which feeds the specialised lending treatment for project, object, commodities finance, income-producing and high-volatility commercial real estate. Consequently, a bank cannot apply a favourable rating-based weight to a specialised lending exposure unless the rating attaches to that exposure under those rules; otherwise the slotting weights and the associated expected loss treatment apply.
