# Definition of Corporate Exposures

A definitional node within SAMA's Minimum Capital Requirements for Credit Risk (v2.1, December 2022) setting out what constitutes a corporate exposure for the internal ratings-based (IRB) asset-class categorisation, and linked in the surrounding material to Pillar 3 disclosure of how corporate versus retail loans are categorised (including non-performing and forborne treatment under SAMA's Rules on Management of Problem Assets, No. 41033343). It matters because asset-class assignment drives which risk-weight function and risk components apply. It binds banks subject to SAMA's credit risk capital framework, including for their regulatory scope of consolidation.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

### [[SAMA — Specialized Lending (SL)|Specialized Lending (SL)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Decide the asset-class classification first: whether an obligor exposure is ordinary corporate or falls into the specialized lending sub-class determines which IRB treatment you may apply. The corporate exposure definition in the SAMA minimum capital framework expressly carves out specialized lending (project, object, commodities finance, income-producing real estate and HVCRE) as a distinct sub-category within the corporate asset class, so the two provisions form a single classification chain. Practically, a facility captured as SL cannot be risk-weighted on generic corporate parameters where the framework directs the supervisory slotting criteria, and the classification must be documented and defensible to SAMA.
