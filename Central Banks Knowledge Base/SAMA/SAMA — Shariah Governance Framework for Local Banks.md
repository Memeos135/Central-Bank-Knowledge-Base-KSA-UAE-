# Shariah Governance Framework for Local Banks

SAMA's Shariah Governance Framework setting the minimum governance requirements for local banks conducting Islamic banking, covering objectives, definitions, allocation of duties across the board, executive management, Shariah committee, compliance, risk management and internal audit, plus Islamic window operations and product development. It aims to contain Shariah non-compliance risk and complements, rather than displaces, other SAMA regulations. It binds local banks licensed under the Banking Control Law that conduct Shariah-compliant banking.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential

- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether a bank's Shariah governance arrangements are merely best practice or an enforceable supervisory requirement, treat them as the latter: the Shariah Governance Framework is issued by SAMA under the authority of the Banking Control Law and its implementing rules, which is the parent statute for licensed banks. That anchoring means the framework's composition requirements — the Shariah committee, internal Shariah audit and related functions — carry the same supervisory and enforcement consequences as other prudential banking rules. For the reader, non-compliance is a supervisory breach against a licensed bank, not a reputational or self-regulatory matter, and the scope follows the Banking Control Law's definition of the institutions caught.

### [[SAMA — Shariah Compliance Function|Shariah Compliance Function]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Treat the Shariah compliance function as one mandated component of the wider Shariah governance framework a bank must establish, not as a standalone discretionary unit. The internal control provision defines the function as ongoing assessment of Islamic activities for Shariah conformity, while the framework instrument sets out the required composition of functions and elements that make the framework effective. For a compliance decision, an assessment of the compliance function must be read against the framework's full architecture — the presence of one function alone does not evidence framework-level compliance.
