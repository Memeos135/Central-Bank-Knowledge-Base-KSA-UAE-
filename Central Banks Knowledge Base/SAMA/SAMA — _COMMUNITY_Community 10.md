# Bank Account Rules

## Why this community

The core Saudi bank account regime: who may open an account, on what documentation, how accounts are operated, frozen, disclosed, dormant-managed and closed — with AML/CTF customer due diligence as the gating condition throughout.

## How members connect

- Strong hub-and-spoke hierarchy: the Rules for Bank Accounts 1644 is the parent instrument and nearly every other member is a chapter or annex elaborating one account category or lifecycle stage.
- Two definitional branches — natural persons (residents, expatriates) and juristic persons — with the juristic branch further subdividing into resident companies, non-profit/endowment, Hajj and Umrah, multilateral and non-resident/foreign-investor categories, each inheriting the general documentation baseline and adding sector-specific evidence.
- AML/CTF and KYC obligations operate as a cross-cutting condition on opening, remote onboarding, operation and freezing, linking this cluster to the sanctions/TFS and AML regimes; identity anchors such as the MOI computer number feed the verification chain.
- Lifecycle and enforcement obligations (freezing, disclosure and enforcement on accounts, inoperative accounts, closure) attach post-onboarding and involve third-party authority requests, a distinct legal basis from customer consent.
- Special-purpose account rules (escrow for real estate development, collection accounts for payment companies, debt crowdfunding risk reporting) bridge into the payments/PSP and finance-company regimes, where account segregation duties sit alongside the banking rules.
