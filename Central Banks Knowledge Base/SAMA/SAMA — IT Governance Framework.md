# IT Governance Framework

SAMA's Information Technology Governance Framework, structured into domains and subdomains (IT governance and leadership, IT risk management, IT operations management, system change management), each stating a principle plus numbered mandatory control requirements. It also sets the compliance mechanics: periodic self-assessment by Member Organizations via questionnaire, SAMA review and audit, a six-level IT governance maturity model, and a formal waiver/compensating-controls route where a control cannot be met. It binds Member Organizations supervised by SAMA.

**Regimes:** governance/risk

### [[SAMA — IT Risk Management|IT Risk Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Treat IT risk management as a required component of the IT governance structure rather than a separate optional discipline: the governance framework identifies IT risk management as one of its domains and relies on the IT risk instrument for the substantive risk identification, treatment and acceptance concepts. Because the defined terms (risk treatment options such as avoidance, mitigation, transfer and acceptance) sit in the risk instrument, they carry the same meaning when applied under the governance framework. For a decision, this means governance-level roles, board oversight and accountability must be evidenced over the IT risk process itself, and inconsistent internal definitions of risk treatment across the two areas would be a finding.

### [[SAMA — SAMA Cyber Security Framework|SAMA Cyber Security Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping an IT governance gap assessment, do not read the IT Governance Framework as the complete control set: it expressly defers cyber security (and business continuity) requirements to the separate Cyber Security Framework. The two are parallel SAMA supervisory frameworks for the same regulated entities, so the reference is a scope-boundary signal rather than a subordination. The practical consequence is cumulative compliance — cyber policy, standards and procedures are assessed against the Cyber Security Framework and its own maturity expectations, and a finding closed under one framework does not evidence compliance with the other.
