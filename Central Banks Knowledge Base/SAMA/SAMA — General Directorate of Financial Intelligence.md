# General Directorate of Financial Intelligence

This node describes the General Directorate of Financial Intelligence under the AML Law: its status as the national central agency for receiving, analysing and disseminating suspicious transaction reports, its operational independence under the President of State Security, confidentiality duties of its personnel, and its powers to exchange information with domestic competent authorities and foreign counterparts (subject to use-limitation undertakings). Adjacent provisions cover the reporting entity's duty to file detailed STRs and respond promptly to Directorate requests, the tipping-off prohibition, and good-faith reporting immunity, plus cross-border currency/BNI customs declarations. It binds FIs, DNFBPs and NPOs and their boards, management and employees, as well as the Directorate itself.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** For any decision about where and to whom a suspicious transaction report goes, the Anti-Money Laundering Law itself designates the General Directorate of Financial Intelligence as the receiving body, placed under State Security oversight with operational independence. This is an internal reference within the same statute, so the reporting channel is a statutory obligation rather than a supervisory preference. The consequence is that reporting to SAMA or any other regulator does not substitute for filing with the Financial Intelligence unit, and tipping-off and confidentiality constraints attach to that channel.
