# SAMA AML/CTF Guide

The SAMA AML/CTF Guide sets supervisory expectations for financial institutions on anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing programs, elaborating the AML Law, the Law on Combating Terrorism Crimes and its Financing and their implementing regulations. The extracted parts cover governance of the AML/CTF compliance function: senior-management-level appointment of the responsible officer, independence and reporting to the board, an independent specialized AML/CTF unit staffed by Saudi nationals with adequate resources, and board/senior management duties for program design, periodic policy review, training and audit support. It binds SAMA-supervised financial institutions.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, governance/risk

### [[SAMA — Reporting of Suspicious Transactions|Reporting of Suspicious Transactions]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding how to discharge a suspicion trigger identified anywhere in the AML/CTF Guide, you must route the decision through the Guide's own Reporting of Suspicious Transactions section rather than treating detection and reporting as separate duties. The Guide's general compliance obligations expressly point to that section for the reporting procedure, including immediate notification to the SAFIU once suspicion arises and preparation of the supporting technical report. Practically, the internal cross-reference means the timing standard and report content in the reporting section govern, so any escalation or approval step that delays filing beyond the point of suspicion is non-compliant.
