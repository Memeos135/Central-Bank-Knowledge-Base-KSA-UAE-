# Executive Management Responsibilities toward Internal Audit

Covers the third principle of SAMA's Internal Audit Principles for Finance Companies and Real Estate Refinance Companies: the duties of Executive Management (defined as those running day-to-day business and proposing/implementing strategy, i.e. senior management) toward the internal audit function. Relevant to how management must cooperate with, resource, and respond to internal audit without compromising its independence. Binds licensed finance and real estate refinance companies.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, BNPL/finance

- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether management interference has compromised audit independence, this link marks the boundary of what executive management may legitimately do in relation to the internal audit department. The instrument allocates management duties of cooperation, access to information and timely remediation of findings by reference to the department's mandate, while oversight of the function itself sits with the board and audit committee. The consequence is that management may be held accountable for obstructing or failing to act on audit work, but cannot direct audit scope or reporting without breaching the independence requirement.
