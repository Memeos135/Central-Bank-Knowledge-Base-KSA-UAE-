# Internal Models Approach

## Why this community

The internal models approach to market risk capital: the conditions on which a bank may use its own models, the expected-shortfall based capital calculation, and the ongoing model-performance tests (backtesting, P&L attribution, risk factor modellability) that determine continued eligibility.

## How members connect

- Hierarchy runs from the Basel Framework and the IMA general provisions down to detailed model requirements, then to the quantitative tests and the capital aggregation formula.
- Eligibility is conditional and revocable: the risk factor eligibility test splits factors into modellable (capitalised via IMCC/expected shortfall) and non-modellable (capitalised via the stress scenario/stressed ES add-on), while backtesting zones and the PLA test can push a desk back to the standardised approach.
- The P&L definitions (risk-theoretical, hypothetical, actual) and the test metrics (Spearman correlation, Kolmogorov-Smirnov) are shared defined inputs feeding both backtesting and PLA outcomes.
- Several labels appear in near-duplicate form (expected shortfall, PLA test, backtesting zones, IMCC, SES), reflecting the same concepts stated in general provisions and again in the detailed calculation rules — read them as one obligation chain, not separate requirements.
- Liquidity horizons and the IMA default risk model are scope parameters that determine how the expected shortfall and total IMA capital requirement are built up and aggregated.
