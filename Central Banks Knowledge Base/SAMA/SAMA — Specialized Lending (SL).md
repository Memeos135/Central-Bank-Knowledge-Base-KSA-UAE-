# Specialized Lending (SL)

Defines specialized lending (SL) as a sub-set of corporate exposures where repayment depends primarily on the income generated by the financed asset(s) rather than the borrower's broader commercial capacity, and identifies its sub-classes — project finance, object finance, commodities finance and, under IRB, also income-producing real estate and high-volatility commercial real estate. Under the standardized approach, SL exposures use issue-specific external ratings only (issuer ratings are excluded); under IRB, supervisory slotting criteria and slotting risk weights may apply. Binds banks calculating credit risk RWA under SAMA's framework.

**Regimes:** banking prudential

### [[SAMA — High-Volatility Commercial Real Estate (HVCRE)|High-Volatility Commercial Real Estate (HVCRE)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If a commercial real estate facility meets the HVCRE description, you are still inside the specialized lending regime but on a separate, more conservative risk-weight track. HVCRE is defined as the financing of commercial real estate exhibiting higher loss-rate volatility and is listed as one of the SL sub-categories, with its own slotting risk weights and expected-loss treatment distinct from PF, OF, CF and IPRE. The consequence is that mis-labelling HVCRE as ordinary IPRE understates capital; the HVCRE/IPRE boundary decision should be evidenced against the definitional criteria in the primary text.
