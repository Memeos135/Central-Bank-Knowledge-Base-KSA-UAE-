# Internal Shari'ah Supervision Committee

Concept node for the Internal Shari'ah Supervision Committee: a body appointed by an Islamic/Takaful institution, composed of scholars specialised in Islamic financial transactions, mandated to supervise independently the institution's transactions, activities and products and confirm Shari'ah compliance across objectives, activities, operations and codes of conduct. Members must satisfy the fit-and-proper eligibility requirements set in applicable regulations, standards and resolutions. Referenced across several CBUAE Shari'ah governance instruments.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, other

### [[CBUAE — External Shari'ah Auditor|External Shari'ah Auditor]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When the external Shari'ah audit encounters a matter not covered by the enumerated sources of Shari'ah provisions, the auditor cannot resolve it on its own authority and must escalate to the institution's Internal Shari'ah Supervision Committee. This is an express obligation chain: the ISSC holds the resolution-issuing competence within the institution, and the external auditor's role is verification against those resolutions, not independent Shari'ah determination. Practically, an audit conclusion on a novel product or structure is only defensible if an ISSC resolution was first obtained, so the absence of one is itself a process defect.

### [[CBUAE — Higher Shari'ah Authority|Higher Shari'ah Authority]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Where an internal Shari'ah supervision committee has ruled on a product or practice, that ruling does not sit at the top of the chain. Each Islamic financial institution must constitute such a committee, but its determinations operate within, and are subordinate to, the resolutions of the Higher Shari'ah Authority. The decision consequence is hierarchical: an internal committee opinion inconsistent with an Authority resolution cannot be relied on as a compliance defence, and the higher-level resolution should be verified before acting.

### [[CBUAE — Standards Article 9 Classification and Provisioning|Standards Article 9: Classification and Provisioning]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** For an Islamic financial institution applying the staging, classification and provisioning standards, the practical question is who signs off: the prudential rules set the minimum classification and provisioning outcome, while the Internal Shari'ah Supervision Committee must be established within the institution and governs the Shari'ah conformity of the underlying contracts and any restructuring or write-off mechanics used to reach that outcome. The two run in parallel — ISSC approval does not relax a prudential classification (including the transitional counting of months from issuance of the standards), and a prudential requirement does not authorise a Shari'ah-non-compliant instrument. In practice, provisioning decisions on Islamic exposures need both prudential justification and ISSC clearance of the contractual route taken; the reader should confirm the specific provisions in the primary text before relying on this allocation.
