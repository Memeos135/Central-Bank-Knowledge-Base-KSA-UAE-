# CBUAE Guidance for Registered Hawala Providers and LFIs

CBUAE supervisory guidance (issued under Article 44.11 of Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019) setting out expectations for licensed financial institutions dealing with hawala providers and, in a separate part, for Registered Hawala Providers themselves. It covers hawala risk factors (delivery channels, commingled non-hawala business lines, customer base), CDD/EDD on hawala-provider customers, registration-certificate verification, STR filing and permitted/non-permitted RHP services. It binds CBUAE-licensed financial institutions and registered hawala providers; it is guidance that does not override the AML/CFT Law, AML-CFT Decision or CBUAE circulars.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, payments, sanctions/TFS, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — FATF Standards|FATF Standards]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** The hawala guidance is anchored in the FATF standards, so when interpreting what registered Hawala Providers and the Licensed Financial Institutions banking them must do on CDD, transaction monitoring and reporting, the FATF expectations for money or value transfer services inform the intended meaning. The guidance is the supervisory articulation of those standards under the national AML/CTF framework, not a free-standing source of obligation, so the enforceable duties still rest on the AML law and its implementing regulation. Practically, use the FATF basis to resolve interpretive gaps and to frame risk-based decisions on maintaining or exiting hawala relationships, but cite the domestic obligation when documenting the decision.
