# AML/CFT Guidance on Transaction Monitoring and Sanctions Screening

CBUAE guidance setting supervisory expectations for two linked control pillars: transaction monitoring for ML/TF/PF detection and sanctions screening for targeted financial sanctions compliance. It covers risk assessment, risk-based deployment of manual versus automated controls, data quality and integrity, pre-implementation testing, governance and board/senior-management oversight. Applies to CBUAE-licensed financial institutions; a UAE instrument, useful comparatively rather than as KSA authority.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, sanctions/TFS, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a sanctions screening programme is adequate, the guidance sets the governance frame around it: board or delegated committee oversight, regular reporting on control performance, and review of the financial crime risk assessment. The screening programme is therefore not a standalone technical control but an element the governing body must be able to evidence it supervises, including staff competence and role-specific training. The practical consequence is that deficiencies in screening are attributable upward — weak oversight or untrained screening staff can itself be the finding, independent of whether a specific match was missed.
