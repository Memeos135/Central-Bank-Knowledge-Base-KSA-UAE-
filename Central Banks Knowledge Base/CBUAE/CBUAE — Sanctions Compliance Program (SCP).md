# Sanctions Compliance Program (SCP)

Concept node for the Sanctions Compliance Program (SCP) that CBUAE expects LFIs to develop, implement and keep updated to meet Cabinet Decision 74 obligations and directives of competent and supervisory authorities. It sets the proportionality principle (scope tailored to risk profile, nature, scale, complexity, products, customer base and geographies) and frames the SCP pillars: senior management commitment, risk assessment, risk appetite, internal controls, training, and independent audit. Applies to all CBUAE-licensed/supervised FIs, including payment service providers, finance companies, exchange houses, hawala providers and insurance entities.

**Regimes:** sanctions/TFS, governance/risk, AML/CTF

### [[CBUAE — Internal Controls|Internal Controls]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether an institution's controls are adequate, the internal controls requirements and the sanctions compliance programme are two layers of the same obligation set and should be evidenced together. Internal controls are the generic policy, procedure and process discipline covering the AML/CFT/CPF programme, while the sanctions compliance programme is the specific build-out required to meet targeted financial sanctions obligations under the applicable Cabinet Decision and related directives. The consequence is that a sanctions programme cannot be treated as a standalone bolt-on: gaps in general internal controls will be read as gaps in sanctions compliance, and vice versa.

### [[CBUAE — Record Keeping|Record Keeping]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Where an institution departs from its own AML/CFT policy or applies compensating controls, the record-keeping rules make the documented rationale itself a retainable record subject to the statutory retention period, and the sanctions compliance programme is one of the frameworks whose design, updates and exceptions must be evidenced. The two instruments form an obligation chain: the SCP sets the substantive duty to build and maintain sanctions controls under the relevant Cabinet Decision and competent authority directives, while record keeping determines how long that evidence must survive for supervisory review. Consequence: an SCP decision is only defensible if the underlying rationale and approvals are retained to the record-keeping standard, not merely taken.
