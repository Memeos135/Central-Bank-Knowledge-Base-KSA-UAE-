# Risk-Based Approach

Cross-cutting concept node describing the risk-based approach as applied across several CBUAE AML/CFT guidance documents, including the hawala guidance for licensed financial institutions and for RHP. It covers the three components of an RBA — enterprise risk assessment (Art. 4.1 AML-CFT Decision), customer risk profiling and calibrated preventive measures — and requires higher-risk customer types such as hawala providers to be reflected in the assessment and documented. It binds LFIs and, in proportionate form, RHP, whose risk assessments must be documented and explainable to the CBUAE.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** For any decision about how much due diligence or monitoring is enough, the risk-based approach is legally anchored in the AML-CFT Law and Decision rather than being a discretionary methodology the institution may adopt or drop. That means customer risk rating and the institutional risk assessment are the mechanism by which the Decision's identification-and-assessment obligation is satisfied, and the institution bears the burden of showing its risk judgements are documented and defensible. ### [[CBUAE — Licensed Financial Institution (LFI)|Licensed Financial Institution (LFI)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding how much due diligence a given customer warrants, the starting point is that the obligation attaches to the entity because it is a Licensed Financial Institution, and the calibration standard is the risk-based approach rather than a fixed checklist. The LFI definition identifies the obligor; the risk-based approach provisions then require that preventive measures be set by reference to a documented customer risk rating and the institution-wide risk assessment, expressly including legal persons and arrangements. Practically, an LFI cannot defend uniform or default-level CDD: it must be able to show the risk rating and the institutional assessment that justify the measures applied to that customer.
