# CBUAE Guidance for the Payment Sector (Non-Bank PPS)

CBUAE guidance setting out ML/FT risk understanding and expected preventive measures for the payment sector, including non-bank payment service providers, SVF providers, card schemes and banks acting as issuers or acquirers. It analyses risk drivers (transaction speed, limits, open vs closed loop, funding and cash access, cross-border movement, regulatory gaps, intermediation, nesting, agents) and then prescribes control expectations split between institutions serving customers/merchants directly and those servicing other payment-sector participants, plus targeted financial sanctions obligations. Non-binding in form but states supervisory expectations for demonstrating compliance with the AML-CFT Law, AML-CFT Decision and Cabinet Decision 74.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, payments, sanctions/TFS, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — Customer Due Diligence (CDD)|Customer Due Diligence (CDD)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When you are onboarding or reviewing a payment-sector participant, the generic CDD/EDD/SDD framework is the operative obligation, but the payment-sector guidance tells you what adequate diligence actually looks like for that customer type. The link is an obligation chain: the CDD provisions set the duty (identify, verify, risk-rate, keep updated, and control any reliance on third parties), while the payment guidance elaborates the expectation that the institution understand how its payment-sector customer uses the service, including whether that customer is intermediating for its own downstream customers or transacting proprietarily. Practically, a file that satisfies baseline CDD but does not evidence understanding of the customer's underlying payment flows and customer base is unlikely to be defensible; treat the nested-customer dimension as part of the risk assessment feeding EDD decisions.
