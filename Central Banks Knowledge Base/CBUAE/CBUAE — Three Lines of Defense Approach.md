# Three Lines of Defense Approach

Concept node for the three lines of defence model as reflected in CBUAE risk governance requirements: business line management owning and operationalising risk limits, an independent risk management function headed by the CRO plus a compliance function, and internal audit providing independent assurance reporting to the board audit committee. It describes the mandatory minimum elements of a bank's risk governance framework, which must be documented and board-approved and may be scaled to the bank's risk profile, nature, size and complexity. Binds CBUAE-licensed banks and their boards.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential

- **What this link tells you:** In judging whether a board-approved risk governance framework is compliant, the three lines of defence structure is a mandatory design element of that framework rather than a separate best-practice option. The risk governance article sets the breadth of risks the framework must address, including concentration, IRRBB, liquidity, reputational, compliance, legal and strategic risk, while the linked standard requires that framework to allocate responsibility across business line management, independent risk and compliance functions, and an independent internal audit. The consequence is that gaps in independence or in audit effectiveness are governance breaches in their own right, and the three lines must demonstrably cover the full risk inventory, not just credit and market risk.

### [[CBUAE — Risk Governance Framework|Risk Governance Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If the question is whether a compliance or risk function is adequately structured, the three lines of defence model is not optional design preference but a mandated component of the board-approved risk governance framework. The framework definition establishes board ownership of risk strategy and appetite, and the linked requirement specifies that this framework must incorporate business-line senior management, the risk management and compliance control functions, and an independent and effective internal audit. Consequently, arrangements that blur the control functions into the first line, or that leave internal audit without genuine independence, are a deficiency in the framework itself and not merely in a single function.
