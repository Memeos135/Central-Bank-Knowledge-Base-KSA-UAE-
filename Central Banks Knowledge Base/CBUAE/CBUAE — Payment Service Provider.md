# Payment Service Provider

Core regulated-entity concept of the CBUAE Retail Payment Services and Card Schemes Regulation: the licensed person providing one or more of the nine categories of Retail Payment Services (payment account and payment instrument issuance, merchant acquiring, aggregation, fund transfer, payment token and payment information/initiation services). The node's context covers the conduct side — liability and refund duties for Unauthorized Payment Transactions, timing of refunds and value-dating, the fraud-suspicion carve-out with written notification to the Central Bank, and allocation of loss between account-issuing PSPs and Payment Initiation Service Providers. Binds all licensed PSPs and, indirectly, their Agents.

**Regimes:** payments, consumer protection, governance/risk, AML/CTF

### [[CBUAE — License|License]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Use this link when deciding whether a given entity may lawfully perform a payment activity: the licence concept is defined by reference to the payment service provider category it authorises, so authorisation is activity-specific, not entity-generic. Holding a valid licence for one category (for example token-related services) does not extend to other enumerated retail payment services such as account information services, each of which attaches to its own provider classification. The consequence is that any new or expanded service line must be tested against the licensed category and a variation or separate authorisation sought before launch.

### [[CBUAE — Wages Protection System (WPS)|Wages Protection System (WPS)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** For a decision on whether a provider can participate in wage disbursement infrastructure, note that the wages framework borrows the payment service provider definition from the retail payment services regime, so only entities properly licensed in that category are eligible applicants. Participation then adds a further, cumulative layer of access conditions — notably security and risk-mitigation requirements aimed at protecting the system itself. In practice a licence is necessary but not sufficient: the provider must satisfy the separate participation criteria, and breach of those conditions is an access/eligibility issue distinct from its underlying licensing status.
