# Risk Management Regulation 153/2018

The instrument itself: CBUAE Risk Management Regulation, Circular No. 153/2018 dated 27 May 2018, addressed to all banks. It sets an overarching prudential framework for risk management — Board and Senior Management oversight, the risk management function alongside internal audit and compliance as independent control functions, risk appetite and limits, measurement and models, stress testing, information systems and disclosure — with accompanying Standards. Risks-specific standards and supervisory expectations sit in other CBUAE regulations; this is the umbrella governance instrument aligned to leading international practice.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential

### [[CBUAE — Risk Management Function|Risk Management Function]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Where the Risk Management Regulation prescribes substantive measurement expectations (for example the categorisation of behavioural and automatic option-risk triggers in banking-book products), the risk management function is the body obliged to operationalise them. The link is an obligation chain: the regulation sets what must be measured, while the function-level requirement demands a feedback loop so that changes in measured risk actually drive changes to risk policy, risk limits and mitigation. For a compliance decision, evidence of measurement alone is insufficient — you must be able to show that outputs fed back into limit-setting and policy revision.

### [[CBUAE — Stress Testing of Material Risks|Stress Testing of Material Risks]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a stress testing programme, do not limit it to the risks named in the capital adequacy and liquidity rules; the risk management regulation requires all other material risks to be captured. The stress testing provision operates as an obligation chain within the same regulation, taking the risk taxonomy identified elsewhere in that instrument, such as option, behavioural and repricing risks, and requiring them to be tested where material. The practical consequence is that materiality assessment, not the published risk list, determines programme coverage, and an institution must be able to justify any risk it excludes.
