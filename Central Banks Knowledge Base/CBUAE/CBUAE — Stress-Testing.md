# Stress-Testing

A concept node for the stress-testing article within the CBUAE Country and Transfer Risk Standards (154/2018), which sits between the risk management function requirements and the information systems/reporting articles. It concerns the requirement for banks to test the resilience of cross-border and country exposures under adverse scenarios and to feed results into governance, limits and provisioning decisions. It binds banks with country and transfer risk exposure, with intensity proportionate to risk profile, nature, size and complexity.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** If you are deciding whether an institution's stress-testing programme is adequate, read it as an output of the risk management system rather than a standalone exercise. The risk management standard enumerates the risk domains that must be covered — underwriting and reserving, asset–liability management, investment including derivatives, liquidity and concentration, and operational risk — while the stress-testing standard frames the risk governance framework through which the board sets strategy, articulates risk appetite and monitors adherence. In practice, a stress-testing scope that omits a domain the risk management system requires to be covered is a defensible finding, and board-level risk appetite is the common anchor for both. Note the two sit in different prudential regimes (insurance-oriented risk management versus bank-oriented stress testing), so check sectoral applicability in the primary text before transposing requirements.
