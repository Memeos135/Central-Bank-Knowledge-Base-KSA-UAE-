# Standard Re Liquidity at Islamic Banks

CBUAE Standard on Liquidity at Islamic Banks, issued under the Central Bank Law (Decretal Federal Law No. 14 of 2018) and forming part of, and read together with, the Regulation re Liquidity at Banks. It sets legally binding qualitative liquidity risk management and governance expectations, quantitative ratios (ELAR, ASRR, LCR, NSFR) and reporting duties, calibrated for Shari'ah-compliant business. It binds licensed banks conducting all their activities per Islamic Shari'ah (Islamic Banks); banks with an Islamic Window remain under the conventional liquidity regime but must report stand-alone measures for monitoring only.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — Higher Shari'ah Authority (HSA)|Higher Shari'ah Authority (HSA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a liquidity instrument or buffer qualifies for an Islamic bank, two gates apply, not one: the Standard forms part of the wider Regulations re Liquidity at Banks, so the prudential eligibility test stands, while the instrument must also sit within the Shari'ah parameters set by the HSA. The reference imports the HSA's role as the body whose rules, principles and standards frame Shari'ah compliance for the institution's activities. Practically, a treasury instrument that is prudentially acceptable can still be unusable if it falls outside HSA-set parameters, so liquidity planning decisions need Shari'ah sign-off as a parallel condition rather than a later step.
