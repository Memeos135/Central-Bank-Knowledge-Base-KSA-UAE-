# Suspicious Transaction Reporting

## Why this community

The detection-to-reporting leg of AML/CTF: transaction monitoring, internal escalation, and mandatory suspicious transaction/activity reporting to the FIU, plus the governance, training and confidentiality duties that support it.

## How members connect

- Monitoring and red-flag indicators feed a reporting obligation: suspicion triggers an STR/SAR filed with the Financial Intelligence Unit through the goAML channel, with the Compliance Officer/MLRO as the accountable filer.
- Tipping Off is the confidentiality constraint attaching to the same reporting event — it limits customer communication and account-handling decisions after suspicion arises.
- Three-lines-of-defence roles allocate the obligation: business-line detection, compliance escalation and filing, and independent audit assurance, each mapped to role-based training and training needs assessment.
- Board and senior management carry ultimate responsibility for the reporting framework; counterfeit currency reporting is a parallel, separately triggered notification duty routed to the same authority.
- Terminology and platform names differ across source instruments (STR vs SAR; goAML variants; UAE FIU references) — align the label to the instrument and jurisdiction actually being applied.
