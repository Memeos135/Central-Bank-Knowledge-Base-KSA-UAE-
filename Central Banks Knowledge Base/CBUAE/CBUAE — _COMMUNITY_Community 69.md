# AML/CFT Guidelines & Typologies

## Why this community

The AML/CFT obligation stack for financial institutions — primary law, implementing decision, supervisory guidance — plus the international standard-setting bodies and typology material (notably trade-based money laundering) that inform risk assessment.

## How members connect

- Clear hierarchy: the AML/CFT primary law sits above the implementing Cabinet Decision, which is in turn operationalised by the supervisory Guidelines for Financial Institutions.
- FATF (with MENAFATF and the Egmont Group) is the common upstream reference used to interpret standards; guidance cites them rather than creating independent obligations.
- Institution-level duties — staff screening and training, group-wide oversight of branches and subsidiaries — trace directly back to specific articles of the Decision and Law.
- Typology annexes (TBML red flags, vulnerable economic sectors) feed the risk-based approach: they shape risk ratings and suspicious-transaction judgement but are indicative, not prescriptive lists.
- The Glossary ties the whole cluster together by fixing defined terms used across law, decision and guidance.
