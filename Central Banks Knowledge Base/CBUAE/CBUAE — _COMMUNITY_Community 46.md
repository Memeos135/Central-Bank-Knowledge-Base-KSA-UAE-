# Counterparty Credit & CVA Risk

## Why this community

Capital treatment of counterparty credit risk arising from derivatives and securities financing, including exposures to central counterparties, the CVA add-on, and the look-through regime for banks' equity investments in funds. The problem-space is how much regulatory capital a bank must hold for risks it takes through counterparties, clearing arrangements and fund structures.

## How members connect

- The Counterparty Credit Risk Standard is the anchor obligation; the CVA Standard and the Equity Investments in Funds Standard both route back to it for exposure measurement, so a change in CCR measurement propagates into CVA and fund capital.
- QCCP is a qualified sub-set of the defined term CCP, and qualification status drives the concessionary treatment of trade exposures and default fund contributions.
- The EIF hierarchy (LTA, then MBA, then FBA) is a mandatory cascade: the bank must use the most granular approach for which it has sufficient information, with the leverage adjustment applied to the resulting risk weight.
- CVA Hedges and CVA Capital Calculation are sub-components specified within the CVA Standard rather than free-standing rules.
