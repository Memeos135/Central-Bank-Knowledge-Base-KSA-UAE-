# Falsely Described Goods and Services

A TBML typology in CBUAE's trade-based money laundering guidance covering misdescription of the nature, type or quality of goods and services in trade and shipping documents, so that value is transferred while documents appear conforming. The guidance flags this as hard to detect and links it to proliferation financing where controlled items are substituted with non-controlled descriptions, and notes that documentary credit checks confirm document conformity rather than the true goods. Relevant to licensed financial institutions handling letters of credit, document examination and related trade payments; CBUAE, not SAMA, source.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, sanctions/TFS

### [[CBUAE — Trade-Based Money Laundering|Trade-Based Money Laundering]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a trade transaction, misdescription of goods or services is not a standalone typology but a sub-category of the trade-based money laundering red flags set out in the parent guidance, so it must be tested against the same TBML controls rather than a separate framework. The link matters because the guidance expressly extends TBML thinking beyond physical goods to service-based schemes, where there is no shipment or customs trail to verify value against. Practically, a licensed institution cannot treat an invoice for services as lower risk merely because documentary verification is harder; it should apply TBML-calibrated scrutiny of pricing plausibility and counterparty rationale.
