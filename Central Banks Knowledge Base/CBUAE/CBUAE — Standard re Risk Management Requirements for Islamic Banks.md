# Standard re Risk Management Requirements for Islamic Banks

The CBUAE Standard on risk management requirements for Islamic banks forms part of the Risk Management Regulation (Circular 153/2018) and is mandatory and enforceable in the same way. It applies to Islamic banks and banks housing an Islamic Window (for their Shari'ah-compliant business, integrated into the existing risk framework) and addresses risks specific to Islamic finance — Shari'ah non-compliance, displaced commercial, rate of return and equity investment risk — plus governance roles for the Board, Risk Committee, ISSC, internal Shari'ah compliance and internal Shari'ah audit. It supplements rather than replaces the other CBUAE risk management standards.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential, other

### [[CBUAE — Higher Shari'ah Authority (HSA)|Higher Shari'ah Authority (HSA)]] — `references` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** For an Islamic bank, risk appetite is a layered obligation: the baseline risk management standard applies, and the Islamic-specific standard adds mandatory coverage of Shari'ah non-compliance risk, displaced commercial risk, rate of return risk and equity investment risk, with the Higher Shari'ah Authority setting the rules, principles and standards against which Shari'ah compliance is assessed. This means Shari'ah non-compliance is treated as a supervised risk category, not solely an internal religious governance matter, and the internal Shari'ah committee's determinations must sit within HSA parameters. In practice, an appetite statement that meets only the conventional minimum is deficient for an Islamic bank; verify the operative provisions of both standards before concluding.
