# AML/CFT Program

A concept node describing the required AML/CFT Program for Licensed Exchange Houses: documented policies and procedures communicated to staff, plus the governance architecture of a dedicated Compliance Officer (Article 21 of the AML-CFT Decision; Paragraph 16.4 of the Standards), an Alternate Compliance Officer and a Compliance Committee. The Compliance Officer role must be ring-fenced to AML/CFT tasks to avoid conflicts of interest, and must have adequate resources and autonomy. Binds LEH management and boards; the program's content is driven by the entity's risk assessment findings.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — Transaction Monitoring|Transaction Monitoring]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If you are assessing whether a licensed exchange house's AML/CFT program is adequate, transaction monitoring is not a separate optional control but a component the program must document and operationalise. The program obligation is anchored in the AML-CFT Law and Decision and the related standards, and monitoring supplies the ongoing, retrospective view of customer activity that lets the institution detect deviations from expected behaviour and feed suspicion reporting. Practically, a gap in monitoring design, calibration or escalation is chargeable as a deficiency in the program itself, not merely a detection failure.
