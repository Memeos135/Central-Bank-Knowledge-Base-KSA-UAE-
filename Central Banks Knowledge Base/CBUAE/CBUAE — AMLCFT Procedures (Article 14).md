# AML/CFT Procedures (Article 14)

Article 14 of the CBUAE Stored Value Facilities Regulation sets the AML/CFT expectations for SVF Licensees, including due diligence models calibrated to the Customer profile and SVF product features, compliance management arrangements enabling AML/CFT system implementation, senior management oversight, and appointment of a Compliance Officer and a Money Laundering Reporting Officer. Licensees must maintain comprehensive AML/CFT policies and procedures under the applicable AML/CFT law and regulations, and must risk-assess and mitigate risks before launching new products, services or technology; FATF risk-based guidance on virtual assets and VASPs is referenced. Binds SVF Licensees (and licensed banks conducting SVF business).

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, payments, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** For an SVF licensee, product design decisions and AML/CFT obligations are one decision, not two: the SVF Regulation treats higher maximum stored value or transaction limits as a driver of ML/TF risk, and its AML/CFT provision requires policies and procedures that comply with the separate AML/CFT law and regulations. This is a cross-regime chain — the prudential/licensing instrument imports the AML regime rather than restating it, so the AML statute's defined customer, CDD and reporting duties apply in their own terms. Consequently, any decision to raise limits or loosen onboarding must be justified against both the SVF conditions and the AML/CFT framework, and breaches can be pursued under either.
