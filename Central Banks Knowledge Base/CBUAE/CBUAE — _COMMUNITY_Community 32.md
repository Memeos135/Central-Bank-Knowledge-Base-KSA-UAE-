# Exchange Business Standards

## Why this community

Prudential and AML/CFT conduct standards for exchange houses and money remitters: licensing and appointment approvals, the CID/KYC/CDD sequence, wire transfer originator/beneficiary information, third-party transactions, screening, record retention, training, unclaimed funds and complaints handling.

## How members connect

- The exchange business standards act as the umbrella instrument, cross-referencing each operational obligation and citing the AML/CTF law as its statutory anchor.
- Definitional layering: customer identification is the narrower verification step inside KYC, which in turn sits inside the broader risk-based CDD obligation — conflating them misstates the required depth of enquiry.
- Third-party transactions link back to KYC and to sanctions screening: acting for a non-customer triggers additional identification and screening duties rather than reliance on the presenting party alone.
- Licensing/appointment forms (licensed person, authorised person appointment, foreign account) form a fit-and-proper approval chain that gates who may operate or be appointed.
- Wire transfer information, record retention and training are the standing controls that make CDD outputs usable for later investigation and supervisory review.
