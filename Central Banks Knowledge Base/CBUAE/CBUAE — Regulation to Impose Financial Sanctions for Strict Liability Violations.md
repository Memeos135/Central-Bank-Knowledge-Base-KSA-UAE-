# Regulation to Impose Financial Sanctions for Strict Liability Violations

The Regulation to Impose Financial Sanctions for Strict Liability Violations (Circular 22/2021) creates a regime under which fixed financial penalties are applied automatically to Licensed Financial Institutions for defined, largely routine regulatory breaches, without further enquiry, representations or appeal (force majeure aside). It rests on the sanctioning powers in the Central Bank Law and covers obligations such as currency handling, court order processing, data reporting and customer certificates. It binds all LFIs, including national banks, foreign bank branches, exchange houses, finance companies and other financial institutions.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential, other

- **What this link tells you:** Where the issue is exposure to automatic penalties, note that the strict-liability financial sanctions regulation is itself issued under the 2018 Central Bank Law, so the Law is the source of the power to impose monetary sanctions and of any appeal or mitigation route. Because liability is framed as strict, the absence of intent or of harm is generally not a defence, and the analysis shifts to whether the triggering conduct occurred and whether the sanction was validly imposed under the Law. Advise clients to focus on factual occurrence and procedural challenge, and to read the sanction-setting provisions of the Law alongside the regulation.
