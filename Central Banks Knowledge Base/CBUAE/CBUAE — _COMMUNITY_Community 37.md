# Bank Capital Adequacy (Basel III)

## Why this community

Prudential capital adequacy for banks under the Basel III construct: what qualifies as regulatory capital, the deductions and adjustments applied to it, the buffers layered on minimum ratios, and the distribution and exposure-concentration consequences of falling short.

## How members connect

- Anchored on a shared defined-term hierarchy of capital tiers (CET1, AT1, T2 feeding Tier 1 and total capital supply), with regulatory adjustments, threshold deductions, DTA and minority-interest treatment operating as corrections to the same base definitions.
- Obligation chain runs from the Basel III framework into the domestic capital adequacy regulation and its implementing circular (Circular 52/2017), then into specialist standards such as the leverage ratio standard, which cites the regulation and reuses its capital definitions.
- Buffer and distribution mechanics are interlocking: conservation and countercyclical buffers are met in CET1, and buffer shortfalls bite through the Maximum Distributable Amount; D-SIB designation and PONV/loss-absorption terms add systemic and instrument-level conditions.
- Exposure-side members (large exposure definition, group of connected counterparties, sovereign/PSE and related-party exposures) are calibrated as percentages of the capital base, so they inherit the capital definitions; transitional grandfathering and amortisation govern legacy instruments.
