# Point of Non-Viability (PONV)

Defines Point of Non-Viability (PONV) and the related "Non-Viable" trigger for Tier capital instruments: the Central Bank's determination that a bank has or will become non-viable absent a write-down of principal or a public capital injection. It also frames the eligibility/grandfathering process for existing AT1 and Tier 2 instruments, including when a Central Bank letter activating a conditional PONV clause is required and the need for external legal certification. Binds banks issuing or grandfathering regulatory capital instruments under CBUAE capital standards.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether a capital instrument's loss-absorption trigger has been reached, the decision is not the bank's to make: the PONV definition vests that determination in the Regulator, i.e. the Central Bank as the competent authority for the institution. The link is therefore an authority-attribution one — the defined term embeds the supervisor as the decision-maker for write-down or public-support-dependency findings. Practically, treat PONV clauses as supervisor-triggered, and confirm in the primary text which regulator is named for the instrument in question, since group or cross-border issuance can involve a home-state authority.
