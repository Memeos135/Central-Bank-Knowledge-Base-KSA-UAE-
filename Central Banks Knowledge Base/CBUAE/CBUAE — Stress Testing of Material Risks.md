# Stress Testing of Material Risks

Node on stress testing and internal risk reporting under Risk Management Regulation 153/2018: banks must run a stress-testing programme proportionate to their risk exposures and feed results on an ongoing basis into risk management, contingency planning and internal capital and liquidity assessment. Internal capital/liquidity adequacy processes must reflect the nature and level of risks taken, covering all material risks beyond those specified in the Capital Adequacy and Liquidity Regulations and Standards. It also requires information systems capable of measuring and reporting exposures bank-wide and group-wide across risk types, products and counterparties, with timely reporting to Board and Senior Management in usable formats.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — Risk Management Regulation 1532018|Risk Management Regulation 153/2018]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a stress testing programme, do not limit it to the risks named in the capital adequacy and liquidity rules; the risk management regulation requires all other material risks to be captured. The stress testing provision operates as an obligation chain within the same regulation, taking the risk taxonomy identified elsewhere in that instrument, such as option, behavioural and repricing risks, and requiring them to be tested where material. The practical consequence is that materiality assessment, not the published risk list, determines programme coverage, and an institution must be able to justify any risk it excludes.
