# AML-CFT Decision (Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019)

Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019, the implementing regulation of the UAE AML-CFT Law, which operationalises the statutory duties — including the suspicious transaction reporting process, confidentiality and tipping-off controls, and the liability protection for good-faith reporters. It sits between the Law and CBUAE supervisory guidance and binds subject entities, notably Licensed Financial Institutions. Consult it for the detailed procedural requirements behind a reporting or CDD obligation stated in general terms in the Law.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, sanctions/TFS

### [[CBUAE — Customer Due Diligence (CDD)|Customer Due Diligence (CDD)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** For any question about the scope of CDD, the operative legal duty comes from the AML-CFT Law and Decision; the guidance chapter tells you how the tiers apply in practice. That means EDD and SDD are not discretionary service levels but calibrations of a statutory obligation, and the same applies to periodic review, refresh of customer information, and reliance on third parties. ### [[CBUAE — MLFT Risk Assessment|ML/FT Risk Assessment]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding how granular an institution's enterprise risk assessment must be, treat the enumerated risk factors as elaboration of the Decision's risk-based obligation rather than as guidance you may narrow at will. The AML-CFT Decision sits above the guidance as the parent instrument, so the listed dimensions — customer, product/service, delivery channel, new technologies, geography, counterparty and other residual areas — define the minimum coverage supervisors will test against. Omitting a category, or assessing it only implicitly, exposes the institution to a finding that its risk-based controls are unsupported at source.
