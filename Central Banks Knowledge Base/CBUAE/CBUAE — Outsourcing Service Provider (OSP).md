# Outsourcing Service Provider (OSP)

Node on the Outsourcing Service Provider (OSP) as an actor under the CBUAE enabling-technologies guidelines, principally in cloud arrangements. It covers the due diligence expected before engagement — verifying the provider's maturity, adequacy and appropriateness against the intended use, with depth proportionate to the materiality of the arrangement and the degree of reliance — the clear allocation of roles and responsibilities between Institution and OSP, contract management and review, and record-keeping of due diligence and contractual documentation. The obligations rest on the Institution, not the OSP; the provider is the object of assessment and contractual control.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, other

### [[CBUAE — Cloud Computing|Cloud Computing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Before contracting for cloud services, the decision to be made is whether the arrangement is an outsourcing — because if a third party supplies the cloud capability, the counterparty is an Outsourcing Service Provider and the full pre-contract due diligence expectations attach. The cloud provisions expressly borrow the OSP due diligence process rather than creating a separate, lighter standard, so maturity, adequacy and appropriateness of the provider and the selected services must be verified on the same basis. Consequence: a cloud procurement assessed only as a technology purchase, without OSP-grade due diligence and ongoing oversight, will not meet the requirement.
