# Sanctions Screening Compliance

## Why this community

The design and governance of a sanctions compliance programme and its supporting transaction monitoring function - what a supervised institution must have in place to detect listed persons and suspicious activity, and how that framework is tested and supervised.

## How members connect

- Programme hierarchy: the Sanctions Compliance Program is the umbrella obligation, with senior management commitment, risk assessment, risk appetite, policies and procedures, training and independent audit/testing as its mandated components.
- Guidance layer: the AML/CFT transaction monitoring and sanctions screening guidance interprets the underlying obligations and pulls the risk assessment and monitoring programme elements into a single expected standard.
- Legal anchor: the terrorism-list decision supplies the designation source that screening must operate against, converting programme design into a concrete listing obligation.
- Supervisory feedback loop: thematic review findings on screening testing inform risk appetite calibration and the adequacy of independent testing.
- Note the regime distinction - sanctions/TFS obligations are strict and list-driven, whereas AML/CTF monitoring is risk-based; a single system supports both but the legal triggers differ.
