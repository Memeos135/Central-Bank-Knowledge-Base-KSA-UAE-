# Economic Sectors Vulnerable to TBML

Risk-assessment section of the CBUAE TBML guidance identifying economic sectors and products most exposed to trade-based laundering, including gold, precious metals and minerals, and construction materials, and explaining the shared traits of wide pricing margins, long supply chains and goods that are hard for customs to inspect. It supports risk rating of trade customers and products by LFIs. Informative for LFIs' business risk assessment and customer risk profiling rather than imposing a discrete obligation.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, sanctions/TFS

- **What this link tells you:** The sector vulnerability material operates as the risk-segmentation input to the TBML framework: it tells an institution which customer and product portfolios should attract heightened trade-related scrutiny. Because the guidance derives these sectors from FATF work spanning both high-value/low-volume and low-value/high-volume goods, sector exposure is a legitimate factor in a risk-based approach rather than an optional overlay. In practice, an institution's TBML risk assessment and transaction monitoring calibration should be defensible by reference to the sectors it actually serves.
