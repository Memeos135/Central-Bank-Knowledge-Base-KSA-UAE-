# Grievances & Appeals Committee

Concept node for the Grievances & Appeals Committee, the statutory review body established under the Central Bank Law to which any person aggrieved by specified Central Bank decisions (such as licensing, suspension or revocation decisions concerning large-value payment system operators and settlement institutions) may refer the decision. Referral must be made in writing to the Central Bank stating the grounds for review, in accordance with the committee's charter. It binds/serves any Person affected by the listed decisions and frames the administrative appeal route within the LVPS regulation.

**Regimes:** payments, governance/risk, other

- **What this link tells you:** When deciding how a supervised entity can challenge a Central Bank decision, the appeal route is not a creature of the regulation itself but of the parent statute: the regulation's defined term for the Grievances & Appeals Committee simply points back to Article 136 of the Central Bank Law. That means the Committee's composition, mandate, time limits and finality of its determinations must be read from the Law, not inferred from the regulation. Practically, any grievance or escalation strategy should be built on the statutory provision, and the regulation used only to confirm which decisions are routed to that Committee.
