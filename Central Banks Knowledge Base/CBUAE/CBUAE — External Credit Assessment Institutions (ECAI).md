# External Credit Assessment Institutions (ECAI)

A concept node on the use of external credit assessments (ECAI ratings) within CBUAE capital standards, covering when an external or inferred rating may drive risk weights for securitisation and other exposures. It sets recognition conditions — inferred ratings must reflect subordination and maturity of the reference exposure and be kept current — and bars reliance on assessments that rest on the bank's own unfunded support or that already embed credit risk mitigation, to prevent double counting. Binds banks subject to CBUAE capital adequacy standards when assigning risk weights.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

- **What this link tells you:** When selecting a rating source to set standardised risk weights, the operative test is regulator recognition, not market reputation: only ECAIs recognised as eligible by the Central Bank under its published recognition criteria may be used. The link to the Basel Committee reflects that ECAI is a Basel-derived defined term and that the local recognition criteria implement BCBS standards, which is why the abbreviation is carried through consistently across the rulebook. ### [[CBUAE — External Ratings-Based Approach (SEC-ERBA)|External Ratings-Based Approach (SEC-ERBA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Before a bank can risk-weight a securitisation exposure under the external ratings-based approach, it must confirm that the rating comes from an external credit assessment institution the Central Bank has recognised as eligible for capital purposes. The ECAI eligibility criteria that govern the standardised approach carry through to SEC-ERBA, which is one of only two permitted securitisation approaches alongside SEC-SA. The consequence is binary: an unrecognised or ineligible rating source removes SEC-ERBA as an option and pushes the exposure to the standardised securitisation treatment, typically with a different capital outcome.
