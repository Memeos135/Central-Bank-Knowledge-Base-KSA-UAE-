# AML-CFT Decision

Identifies the AML-CFT Decision (the implementing regulation to the UAE AML-CFT Law) as a core legal basis of the CBUAE CDD/KYC guidance, notably Article 15 on mandatory enhanced due diligence for foreign and domestic PEPs with high-risk factors, their family members and close associates, and for relationships with persons from high-risk countries. The node also records the guidance's status and scope: it supplements, and yields to, the binding legal framework, reflects FATF standards, takes effect on issuance with a one-month compliance window, and applies to all CBUAE-licensed/registered banks, exchange houses, finance companies, payment and payment token service providers, hawala providers, insurers, agents and brokers.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — Enhanced Due Diligence|Enhanced Due Diligence]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding how far to go on a higher-risk customer, treat enhanced due diligence not as a guidance-level good practice but as an obligation traceable to the AML-CFT Decision, which the guidance cites as its source of authority. The guidance elaborates what EDD looks like in practice — source of wealth enquiries, escalated onboarding approvals, and retention of the resulting records — but the enforceable duty sits in the underlying Cabinet Decision. Practically, an institution cannot justify omitting EDD by arguing the guidance is non-binding, and its EDD file must be capable of demonstrating compliance with the parent instrument on inspection.

### [[CBUAE — Ongoing Monitoring|Ongoing Monitoring]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If the question is whether monitoring can be switched off or reduced for a customer after onboarding, the answer is governed by the ongoing monitoring duty in the AML/CFT decision, which the guidance simply applies. That duty attaches to all customers for the life of the business relationship and links continuous CDD/KYC with the trigger for suspicious activity reporting, so monitoring gaps are both a CDD failure and a reporting-risk failure. Decisions to apply simplified measures must therefore be calibrated within that continuing obligation rather than treated as an exemption from it.

### [[CBUAE — Record-keeping|Record-keeping]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** For any record-retention question, the guidance's record-keeping section is not a free-standing rule: it restates duties that sit in the AML/CFT law and in the implementing Cabinet decision. The obligation chain therefore runs from statute to the decision to the guidance, and the enforceable retention periods, scope of records and accessibility requirements come from the higher instruments. When advising on breach or remediation, cite the decision's record-keeping article as the operative source and use the guidance only to show supervisory expectations on how it is met.
