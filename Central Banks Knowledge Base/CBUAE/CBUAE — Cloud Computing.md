# Cloud Computing

Covers the Cloud Computing chapter of the Enabling Technologies Guidelines: materiality assessment, governance framework, auditability, outsourcing to Outsourcing Service Providers, design and business continuity. It sets the test for when a cloud arrangement is 'material' (impact on operations, risk management, regulatory compliance, or confidentiality/integrity of Personal Data) and expects early engagement with the relevant Supervisory Authority before implementing material arrangements. Binds Institutions subject to the Guidelines and interacts with existing outsourcing requirements of each Supervisory Authority.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, data/credit info, other

### [[CBUAE — Enabling Technology|Enabling Technology]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Classifying a cloud arrangement matters because Cloud Computing sits within the Enabling Technology perimeter, and the scope provision captures any use of an Enabling Technology by an institution — including where that use is wholly or partly outsourced. The defined term therefore removes the argument that responsibility shifts to the provider: the institution remains the obligated party for the technology's governance and risk controls. In practice, treat cloud adoption as engaging both the enabling-technology requirements and, where a third party is involved, the outsourcing requirements concurrently.

### [[CBUAE — Outsourcing Service Provider (OSP)|Outsourcing Service Provider (OSP)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Before contracting for cloud services, the decision to be made is whether the arrangement is an outsourcing — because if a third party supplies the cloud capability, the counterparty is an Outsourcing Service Provider and the full pre-contract due diligence expectations attach. The cloud provisions expressly borrow the OSP due diligence process rather than creating a separate, lighter standard, so maturity, adequacy and appropriateness of the provider and the selected services must be verified on the same basis. Consequence: a cloud procurement assessed only as a technology purchase, without OSP-grade due diligence and ongoing oversight, will not meet the requirement.
