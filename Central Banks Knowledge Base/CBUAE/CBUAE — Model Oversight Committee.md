# Model Oversight Committee

Defines the Model Oversight Committee required by the CBUAE Model Management Standards: a dedicated committee, established separately from existing risk management committees, covering all models institution-wide and meeting at least quarterly. It is accountable for substantiated, documented decisions at each step of the model life-cycle, including strategic modelling choices, and must be accountable to Senior Management and the Board while remaining independent of business lines (majority of members not from business lines). It binds institutions subject to the MMS and frames the accountability of model owners, developers, validators, users and modelling data owners.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential

### [[CBUAE — Remediation Process|Remediation Process]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Where a model deficiency is identified, the question of who must act is answered by the same role framework that governs model oversight generally — both provisions sit in one instrument and rely on a common, mandatory allocation of roles (owner, developer, validator, user, data owner, and oversight committee). The remediation process is therefore not a separate workflow but an accountability chain running back to those named roles, with the oversight committee as the escalation and sign-off point. For a compliance decision, unremediated model findings are a governance failure attributable to identified individuals or the committee, not merely a technical shortcoming.

### [[CBUAE — Senior Management|Senior Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If you are allocating responsibility for a model failure, this link fixes the escalation path: the Model Oversight Committee performs the oversight role but answers upward, with Senior Management and the Board carrying the duty to ensure model risk is actually managed to the required standard. Establishing the committee is therefore delegation of function, not of accountability — a defect in model governance remains attributable to the Board and Senior Management. The consequence is that committee terms of reference, membership and reporting lines should be demonstrably approved and monitored at Board/Senior Management level, since that is where a supervisory finding will land.
