# Investment Risk Reserve (IRR)

IRR is a profit-equalization reserve defined in the CBUAE Standard on Profit Equalization for Islamic Banks: an amount appropriated out of Investment Account Holders' income after the Mudarib's profit share, held to absorb future investment losses of IAHs. Islamic Banks (including banks with Islamic Windows) must model its size and periodic build-up, obtain Internal Shari'ah Supervision Committee approval for its establishment, maintenance, deployment and liquidation, disclose it in IA contracts and publicly, and dispose of outstanding balances on voluntary liquidation per agreed terms.

**Regimes:** governance/risk, banking prudential, consumer protection

- **What this link tells you:** Decisions to establish, fund or draw down an Investment Risk Reserve are not purely prudential or accounting decisions — they require Shari'ah governance sign-off. The reserve mechanism exists to smooth investment account holders' returns under profit-sharing structures, and the ISSC is the body charged with confirming that such treatment of participants' funds is consistent with Shari'ah. The consequence is that IRR policy, utilisation criteria and disclosure should be evidenced as reviewed within the Shari'ah governance framework, not approved by finance or risk alone.
