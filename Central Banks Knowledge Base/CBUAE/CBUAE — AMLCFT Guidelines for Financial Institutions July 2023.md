# AML/CFT Guidelines for Financial Institutions July 2023

CBUAE's July 2023 AML/CFT and illegal-organisations guidelines for financial institutions, summarising and elaborating the minimum statutory duties under the UAE AML-CFT Law and its implementing Decision: risk identification and assessment, CDD scope, appointment of a qualified compliance officer, internal controls and MIS, suspicious-transaction indicators, STR filing and cooperation with competent authorities. It is guidance addressed to CBUAE-supervised FIs, which remain solely responsible for compliance with the underlying law, Decision and all notices and circulars. Comparable in function to SAMA AML/CTF rules and guidance for KSA institutions.

**Regimes:** AML/CTF, governance/risk, sanctions/TFS

### [[CBUAE — Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)|Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether to file a suspicion report, staff and board members should treat the safe-harbour protection as attaching to the act of reporting itself, so fear of contractual, civil or criminal exposure is not a valid reason to withhold or delay a report. The guidance ties the reporting duty and the immunity together by grounding both in the parent AML/CFT Law and its implementing Decision, so the protection is a statutory corollary of the obligation rather than a discretionary comfort. Practically, an institution cannot justify non-reporting on confidentiality or liability grounds, and internal escalation policies should reflect that the protection is conditional on reporting in good faith through the prescribed channel — verify the cited articles in the primary text before relying on the scope of the immunity.
