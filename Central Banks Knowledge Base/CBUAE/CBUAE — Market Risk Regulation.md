# Market Risk Regulation

CBUAE prudential regulation on market risk, setting the required market risk strategy and governance framework for banks at entity and, where relevant, group level. It allocates Board responsibility for approving and annually reviewing market risk strategy, policies and processes, requires clear roles, separation of duties and conflict-of-interest avoidance, and obliges Senior Management to implement them; it also mandates policy content such as management information systems, market risk limits aligned to risk appetite and capital strength, exception tracking, model controls, intra-day limit management and trading book allocation, including submission of a trading book policy statement to the Central Bank. It binds banks licensed by the CBUAE.

**Regimes:** banking prudential, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — Risk Management Regulation and Standards|Risk Management Regulation and Standards]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If you are assessing whether a bank's market risk limit framework is adequate, you must satisfy both the general risk management regime and the specialised market risk regime, which state the same core requirement in materially identical terms: limits calibrated to risk appetite, risk profile and capital strength, matched to management capability, and actually communicated to and understood by relevant staff. The overlap means the market risk regulation operates as the detailed expression of a duty already owed under the enterprise-wide risk management framework, so board and senior management accountability runs through both. Practically, a deficiency in market risk limits is simultaneously a governance failure under the risk management regulation, and remediation should be documented against both instruments rather than one.
