# CBUAE Guidance on Implementation of Targeted Financial Sanctions

The CBUAE guidance document on implementation of Targeted Financial Sanctions, issued under Article 44.11 of Cabinet Decision 10/2019, setting out supervisory expectations for how licensed financial institutions demonstrate compliance with UAE TFS obligations. It is to be read with the CBUAE AML/CFT Procedures (Notice 74/2019) and the Executive Office's TFS Guidance for FIs and DNFBPs, and expressly does not create new law — the underlying legal framework prevails in any conflict. It is directed at CBUAE-licensed financial institutions.

**Regimes:** sanctions/TFS, AML/CTF, governance/risk

### [[CBUAE — Sanctions Compliance Program (SCP)|Sanctions Compliance Program (SCP)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** The Sanctions Compliance Program is the vehicle through which the supervisor expects a licensed institution to demonstrate that it discharges its statutory TFS duties. The guidance ties the SCP directly back to the obligations under the Terrorism Lists Regulation and related directives, so the programme is not a voluntary best practice but the evidentiary basis on which supervisory adequacy is judged. In practice, a gap in SCP design, ownership, or update cadence is assessed as a failure to implement the underlying sanctions obligations, not merely as a documentation weakness.

### [[CBUAE — Screening Operations|Screening Operations]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Screening is the operational control by which the TFS guidance expects listing obligations to be given effect, so decisions about screening scope and frequency are sanctions-compliance decisions, not operational preferences. The guidance contemplates screening across the whole customer lifecycle, including periodic, ad hoc and re-screening following list changes, which is what allows freezing to occur without delay after a designation. Consequence: gaps such as screening only at onboarding, or delayed re-screening after list updates, expose the institution to breach of the underlying freezing and notification duties.
